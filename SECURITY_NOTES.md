# Security and side-channel scope

This project currently claims correctness and memory characterization only. It does **not** claim constant-time execution, power-analysis resistance, fault resistance, secure key erasure, or production-ready randomness.

## Published warning

[Simple Power Analysis Attack on SQIsign](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/830),
published at AFRICACRYPT 2025
([DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-97260-7_12](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-97260-7_12)),
identifies secret-dependent behavior in Cornacchia/modular-exponentiation
processing and demonstrates recovery of a secret-derived ephemeral exponent
on a 54-bit toy implementation using a ChipWhisperer Nano/STM32F030. The paper
then connects that leakage to signing-key recovery. The practical trace
experiment is not a full Level-I RP2350 attack, but it invalidates any
assumption that an unprotected SQIsign signer is safe merely because the
protocol is post-quantum.

[Ready to SQI? Safety First!](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/807) separately
studies timing leakage in the half-GCD/Euclidean work around Cornacchia and
shows how leaked sensitive inputs can support key recovery. Consequently,
regularizing only modular exponentiation cannot close the full attack surface.

## Known variable-time surfaces in the frozen signer

- rejection/retry loops in random ideal generation and IdealToIsogeny;
- primality testing, Cornacchia, modular square root, GCD/extended GCD and division;
- LLL/ML2/MLLL lattice reduction and shortest-vector logic;
- C1/libc `qsort` and D1's in-project heap-sort comparison/access behavior;
- early success in `find_uv` list/pair search;
- variable used-limb loops in fixed-precision arithmetic;
- conditional IdealToIsogeny and higher-dimensional isogeny paths;
- challenge/response backtracking and failure exits.

The compact implementation removes GMP but does not thereby become constant-time. Fixed storage width is a useful prerequisite, not a side-channel proof.

## Positive full-Sign structural finding

The complete encoded Sign call has now been screened with host compiler
instrumentation rather than treated as wholly unobserved. Two independent
same-binary processes use a fixed Level-I key/message, alternating pair order,
two fixed/fixed signing-RNG negative controls and four fixed/random pairs. In
both runs the controls have identical signatures, edge streams and
load/store-address streams. All four primary pairs differ in signature, edge
stream and address stream. The raw rows, summaries, binary and analyzer are
hash-pinned, and the summaries are regenerated byte-for-byte by
`scripts/check_sca_control_flow_trace_artifacts.py`.

A separate localization build replaces the nearest-symbol hint with compiler
PC tables and retained LTO debug information. Two clean builds agree for all
four primary comparisons. Seeds 1 and 3 first diverge in the high-zero
used-limb scan at `intbig.c:163-164`, inlined while computing a division
operand size at line 250. Seeds 2 and 5 first diverge in the early-exit
comparison at `intbig.c:992-996`. The raw rows, binaries, DWARF, source hash
and regenerated reports are frozen by
`scripts/check_sca_edge_localization_artifacts.py`.

A second two-clean-build diagnostic captures native caller stacks only at
those eight frozen class-A/class-B events. The used-limb divergence lies on
`ibz_divrem_unsigned_wide` → `ibz_div` → `ibz_div_floor` → `ibz_mod` →
`quat_alg_norm_mod`. The early comparison lies on `ibz_cmp` →
`ibz_rand_interval`. Both then share
`quat_sampling_random_ideal_O0_given_norm_impl` →
`protocols_sign_with_workspace` → `sqisign_sign_with_workspace`. The raw
frames, binaries, DWARF and regenerated reports are frozen by
`scripts/check_sca_first_divergence_stack_artifacts.py`.

This is a positive structural leakage finding for the frozen host corpus. It
does not by itself prove that a physical probe can distinguish the paths, and
it does not observe data values, register Hamming weights/transitions, the
final Cortex-M33 linked image, power, EM or a recovered key. Conversely, the
stable controls mean it cannot be dismissed as an unqualified tracer failure.
Changing only signing randomness also means this experiment establishes
ephemeral-input-dependent control, not leakage of the long-term key. SCA-8
therefore remains fail-closed: the first common remediation boundary is the
complete random-ideal sampler. Replacing only `ibz_cmp` would leave
variable-width norm modular reduction, rejection count, RNG consumption and
first-success publication. Those behaviors need a fixed-width/fixed-round
design with explicit distribution and failure semantics; later divergences
must then be enumerated, the final target trace reviewed, and physical
campaigns completed. No local Cornacchia schedule change can override this
whole-Sign result.

`experiments/sca/random-ideal-constant-work-design.json` freezes the next
design boundary. A read-only twelve-seed LLDB corpus measures the legacy prime
path at gamma 1--4 and beta 1 attempts, but that small corpus is not used as a
tail bound. For the fixed Level-I prime `N=2^512+75`, exact affine point counts
give gamma acceptance above one half and beta failure about `2^-512`. If an
`N^2`-divisible isotropic lift occurs, replacing the first nonzero canonical
coordinate `xi` by `N-xi` changes `Q/N` by `-2*ai*xi mod N`, so a fixed-work
primitive correction removes that rejection without a retry. The protected
budget is therefore 130 gamma candidates and one beta candidate. One fixed
769-bit draw reduced modulo N per coordinate contributes a jointly bounded
coordinate-product distribution distance below `2^-248.37`; outer exhaustion
plus coordinate bias is strictly below `2^-128`.

The first three implementation components now exist: a fixed 97-byte/769-bit
coordinate reduction, a fixed Level-I modular square root and a mask-selected
primitive-lift correction. The square root uses the exact public prime
`N=2^512+75`, its fixed exponent `(N+1)/4=2^510+19`, and a fixed
square/reduction verification. Optimized host, fatal ASan+UBSan and Arm GNU
15.2.1 `-Os`/Pico-like gates pass; the workspaces are
2,808/4,320/6,480 bytes and the public entry frames are 32/48/88 and
32/56/88 bytes respectively. This does not implement the 130 gamma/one beta
schedule, first-success selection, ideal construction or Sign integration.
The frozen branch/address structure also does not mask operand values or prove
constant power. Moreover,
replacing the rare `N^2` rejection by a deterministic correction changes the
sampling map: reduced-domain correctness is not a proof that the output-ideal
distribution satisfies the SQIsign security argument. That distribution proof,
the protected transcript, final Cortex-M33 linked trace and power/EM campaign
remain open. The checker continues to reject constant-time, target and
physical-resistance claims.

## mini-GMP comparison boundary

Bundled mini-GMP stores each `mpz_t` as an allocated-limb count, used-limb
count and limb pointer; its default hooks call `malloc`, `realloc` and `free`.
Replacing those hooks with a static pool removes the system heap, but not the
secret-derived allocation count, requested size, first-fit traversal, block
address or clear traffic. Clear-on-release reduces residual secret lifetime;
it does not make those traces constant-time.

The frozen comparison observes up to 4,830 simultaneously live mini-GMP limb
blocks and tens of millions of allocation events over the 100-vector corpus.
Those are powerful reasons not to import stock mini-GMP into this target
profile, but they are not a leakage attack or an all-input bound. Conversely,
fixed `ibz_t` storage removes the allocator trace only; used-limb loops,
division/GCD, retries and protocol control flow remain variable-time. Neither
backend receives a side-channel-resistance claim.

## Relevant constant-time work

- [Constant-time Integer Arithmetic for SQIsign](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/832) covers required big-integer primitives and reports Timecop/Valgrind validation, but is not a full integrated signer.
- [Constant-time lattice reduction in dimension 4](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/027) provides a tested reduction module with performance costs and practical parameter qualifications.
- [Constant-time Quaternion Algorithms for SQIsign](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2192) regularizes selected HNF/generator/represent-integer/Qlapoti components, while surrounding GMP/lattice or protocol behavior remains outside a full constant-time claim.

These are comparison and future-hardening inputs. The primary low-memory study will finish even if the resulting experimental signer remains variable-time, provided this limitation is explicit.

## Countermeasure dependency order

The machine-readable comparison in
`experiments/sca/published-countermeasure-comparison.json` maps those papers
and the published SPA/timing attacks onto every one of the twelve frozen open
surfaces.  Its checker rejects three shortcuts: treating a fixed schedule as
constant power, treating a constant-time component as a constant-time signer,
or treating Timecop/ctgrind or a negative TVLA screen as physical attack
resistance.

`WP-CTINT-FOUNDATION` has produced the fixed-width component substrate, and a
fixed 1,421-round C-halfgcd now executes on the opt-in experimental Level-I
Cornacchia route.  The selected next package is therefore `WP-CORNACCHIA`:
the frozen source recurrence now has an exact-integer contraction proof showing
that 644 active rounds suffice, leaving 777 scheduled freeze rounds. The next
step is to acquire the already frozen matched legacy-variable image as an
analog positive control and run the preregistered physical mask/state attacks.
This proof is not a formal C/assembly refinement proof. The matched RP2350
timer experiment already detects the known 95-versus-122-step legacy effect
at `+1476.867 us`, whereas the fixed firmware gives `+0.200 us` with an
approximate 95% CI spanning zero.  This validates only the coarse timer
design—not shunt-current or EM sensitivity.  The integration is not promoted to a
constant-time or constant-power result merely because its linked core has one
public loop edge and fixed addresses.

After that component passes bound and physical gates, the order is bounded
quaternion and dimension-4 lattice components, localization and remediation
of the already-positive whole-Sign host trace, final-target structural review,
and complete power/EM plus attack-driven testing.  Strict constant-time
engineering is a release prerequisite for the intended signer, but it is not
sufficient for a power-analysis claim.  Masking or blinding remains a parallel
research gap, not an assumed property of the fixed-width implementation.

### CTINT1728 fixed-width foundation

All fixed-width integer dependency slices now exist as an experimental
component foundation.
They use 54 fixed 32-bit words (1,728 bits, 216 bytes), with caller-owned
432-byte add/subtract, 648-byte shift, and 1,080-byte multiplication
workspaces plus 864-byte exact/floor-square-root, 1,512-byte division,
2,160-byte modular-reduction, 3,240-byte modular-exponentiation, and 3,456-byte
half-GCD workspaces.
Implemented operations are
copy/cmov, zero/sign tests, signed and unsigned comparison, absolute value,
signed and unsigned bit length, lossless unsigned left shift, logical unsigned
right shift, signed add/subtract, signed schoolbook multiply/square, and signed
truncating division, floor-style signed modular reduction, and fixed
521-round square-and-multiply-always modular exponentiation, and fixed
864-round radix-4 exact and floor integer square roots, and a fixed 1,421-round
shift/subtract Cornacchia half-GCD for inputs up to 492 bits.  Division
executes all 1,728 restoring rounds; reduction always executes that division
and a branch-free sign adjustment.  Exponentiation always performs two
multiplications and two complete reductions per round.  Its accepted domain
is a nonnegative exponent of at most 521 bits and a positive modulus of at
most 863 bits, which keeps every residue product inside the signed 1,728-bit
range.  The square-root routine performs all 864 input-pair rounds and
publishes only when the final restoring remainder is zero.  Invalid
shifts, lossy left shifts, signed arithmetic overflow, division by zero, and
the unrepresentable `INT_MIN / -1` quotient leave initialized outputs
byte-identical.  An invalid exponent or modulus still runs all 521 pow rounds
and likewise does not publish an output; negative and nonsquare square-root
inputs similarly run all 864 rounds.

The frozen tests pass independent reference cases, malformed-input and alias
checks and fatal ASan/UBSan.  It exercises 10,000 random add/subtract cases,
2,000 full-width and 2,000 bounded random products, and compares 10,000 input
pairs, 10,000 bounded products, 512 signed divisions, 512 signed reductions,
three modular exponentiations, exact/floor-square-root cases, fixed-half-GCD
boundary/differential cases, and 10,000 shifts
against
the current Level-I `ibz_t`.  The independent component test also covers 100
division boundary pairs, 320 random divisions, the same 100/320 reduction
cases, and four valid plus five invalid exponentiation cases including all
three output/input aliases.  The square-root component adds ten boundary, 32
random exact-square, and 32 adjacent nonsquare cases, including the largest
safe constructed root `2^863-1`.  Arm GNU 15.2.1 emits 3,560 bytes of text for
the `-Os` profile and 4,264 bytes for the Pico-like `-O3`
profile; all component frames are static and at most 80/96 bytes respectively.
The exact checker permits only fixed loop back-edges for scalar decisions and
rejects operand-derived memory addresses or indirect/table branches in the
shift, multiplication, division, reduction, exponentiation, square-root, and
half-GCD
paths.  The
pow gate also confirms exactly 521 rounds, square-and-multiply-always calls,
and an exponent-word address derived only from the public round index.  The
sqrt gate likewise permits only its fixed initialization, limb, and 864-round
back-edges and freezes the public 1726..0-by-two input-address schedule.  The
half-GCD gate freezes 1,421 rounds and permits only the public loop back edge.
An exact-integer source-recurrence proof independently bounds every active
round by a `10/17` squared-norm contraction and derives a 644-round sufficient
bound for positive inputs below `2^492`, leaving 777 freeze rounds.

This is a control-flow/address diagnostic, not a constant-power result or a
formal C/assembly refinement or noninterference proof. In particular, the exponent bit still controls
a data selection and can affect switching activity.  The square-root and
half-GCD subset is wired only into the opt-in experimental Level-I route;
whole-Sign composition, masking, compiled-program refinement, and physical
power/EM testing remain open. Therefore
the published attack is not considered blocked and production signing remains
unapproved.

## Memory-scheduling policy

Allowed scheduling decisions depend only on public compile-time parameters and fixed algorithm phases:

- buffer sizes and offsets are fixed for Level I;
- no allocator is called;
- no buffer address/overlay choice depends on a secret value;
- operation profiles are selected at build time, not from secret data;
- secret-derived candidate vectors, norms, ideals and responses remain in SRAM;
- immutable public constants may reside in flash/XIP;
- flash is not used as swap and never receives ephemeral secret state.

This policy prevents the scheduler itself from introducing secret-dependent layouts. It does not remove leakage from access order, comparisons, retries, or early termination.

## Recompute-vs-store side-channel review

| Candidate | Memory effect | Side-channel concern |
|---|---|---|
| Packed candidate coordinates | Large SRAM reduction | Conversion is fixed-loop; later selected-index access and search are already data dependent |
| Index/permutation sorting | Removes full record copies | Comparison count/access pattern remains data dependent unless replaced by a sorting network |
| Quotient recomputation | Saves 943,488 B in all-row design | Repeats variable-time division; can amplify timing/power leakage |
| Two-row regeneration | Saves all inactive rows | Repeats deterministic secret-derived Gram/norm work; changes trace length and power distribution |
| Stack/workspace overlays | Reduces total SRAM | Safe only when last use is proved; stale secrets must not be observed through aliased typed accessors |

Every accepted optimization must record bytes, cycles, code-size change, correctness effect and constant-time effect in `RESEARCH_LOG.md`. Later constant-time profiles may deliberately choose more memory or cycles.

The implemented packed conversion performs exactly four signed-byte-to-fixed-integer writes and does not choose storage based on secret values. It changes the resident representation and memory traffic, so it may still change power leakage even though no host wall-time slowdown was resolved. No side-channel-resistance claim follows from the 67.7556% block reduction.

## Workspace safety

The implementation should use aligned byte storage plus typed accessors or normal C structs whose lifetimes do not overlap. It must avoid inactive-union type-punning and strict-aliasing violations. Each accessor should enforce:

```text
compile-time size and alignment checks
offset/extent checks in debug/instrumented host builds
one declared owner per phase
explicit initialization before first read
explicit last-use/poison transition in tests
```

AddressSanitizer/UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer host tests should accompany lifetime changes. A workspace sanitizer can fill released ranges and detect unexpected later reads in deterministic tests.

## Zeroization

The frozen compact code securely frees heap blocks, but the final firmware has no heap and must define workspace erasure explicitly. At minimum:

- clear secret-key typed objects on all success/failure exits;
- clear secret-derived `find_uv`, quaternion, lattice and response workspace before returning to the application;
- use a compiler-resistant erasure primitive and inspect generated code;
- do not claim erasure of register files, caches, debug hardware or flash without evidence;
- disable or authenticate production debug access according to the eventual deployment threat model.

Zeroization adds cycles but does not add peak SRAM when performed in place.

Audited C1 commit `e61c1fa…` clears the entire 2,044,256-byte typed `find_uv` workspace at entry and on every normal success, miss or fatal return after finalizing active integers. Validation failure also clears a non-null supplied object. This guarantee does not cover process termination, fault reset, power loss, or illegal aliasing between the workspace and an input/output. A final MCU design must clear the reserved workspace and dedicated PSP on boot/warm reset and must document exclusive, non-reentrant ownership across interrupts and the unused second core.

The explicit C1 layout has fixed parameter-dependent size and addresses, so it adds no secret-dependent allocation decision. It does **not** make the signer constant-time: `qsort`, integer comparison, list search, arithmetic and retries remain variable-time. The host project-code closure has no direct allocator/GMP symbol, but host-libc `qsort` transitive behavior is outside that gate, and current end-to-end K/S still allocates one workspace per `find_uv` invocation through the compatibility wrapper (one observed in KeyGen and two sequentially in Sign). Final Arm firmware must link the valid path against the actual newlib closure while trapping/wrapping allocator entry points.

Audited D1 commit `cf9f6b6…` removes libc `qsort` from the explicit `find_uv` project-code closure and reduces the typed reservation to 1,905,728 bytes. This narrows dependency and memory surfaces, not leakage. The replacement heap sort compares secret-derived full-width norms, and both its comparison order and cycle-application addresses depend on their values. Its trace differs from C1/libc even though twelve deterministic transcripts are byte-identical. D1 therefore remains explicitly non-constant-time. The full host K/S harness still contains `malloc/free` through the compatibility wrapper; only the dead-stripped explicit closure is direct-symbol clean.

Audited D2 commits `00f4290…` and `d680188…` first validate every row norm, then replace the 943,488-byte quotient array with one local 216-byte integer. Buffer size and address remain fixed by the parameter set, and no secret-dependent allocation/layout choice is introduced. The arithmetic trace does change. D1 already has a variable division count because it reserves 4,368 quotient slots per `find_uv` invocation but divides once for each populated row entry before search. D2 instead divides only for visited invertible candidate pairs and stops with the existing first match, adding stronger dependence on invertibility, search progress and the first-match location. Twelve deterministic samples reconstructed 661–748 D1 divisions versus 1–29 D2 divisions in KeyGen, and 1,381–1,502 versus 2–106 in Sign. The no-early-exit D2 per-invocation ceiling is still 9,541,896 candidate pairs before noninvertible exclusions. D2 is therefore a memory result, not a constant-time improvement; changed timing/power correlation and possible SPA amplification remain explicit risks.

Audited D3 commits `f70042d…`, `60ce944…`, and `b54922b…` reduce the
workspace to two fixed row slots. Slot count, size, alignment and addresses
depend only on the Level-I parameter set; diagonal aliasing changes neither
allocation nor placement. Entry and every return path initialize/finalize the
active full-width integers and clear the whole 275,840-byte object. The first
draft crossed a C inner-array boundary while treating the two-dimensional norm
array as flat; independent review caught it, and the frozen nested-loop fix
keeps every access within its declared subarray.

D3 is not a side-channel improvement. It repeats enumeration and secret-
derived norm sorting during Phase 2, and the number of regenerated inner rows
depends on where the inherited first-match search succeeds. The theoretical
row-generation ceiling rises from seven to 35. This can repeat or amplify
timing/power features even though the full candidate stream and protocol bytes
match D2 for the frozen deterministic corpus. Fixed allocation removes no
variable-time arithmetic, comparison, retry or early-exit behavior. No
constant-time or SPA-resistance claim is made; target trace assessment remains
required.

The D2 `dim2id2iso.c` object also contains a host-only regression hook that calls `malloc/free`. It is absent from the currently dead-stripped explicit closure, but that does not prove it will disappear under every static-library/section configuration. The heap-free embedded profile must compile the hook out or move it to a test-only translation unit and must still fail the final ELF if any allocator symbol survives. Relying only on `--gc-sections` is not the intended security boundary.

D4 commit `a6b0628…` moves the four secret-derived VLA families into a typed
77,168-byte lattice-state member. Each active integer/ideal is initialized and
finalized through the inherited two-dimensional loops, and the enclosing
353,008-byte object is cleared on entry and every return path. Independent
review checked early failure and cleanup paths, exact array bounds, alignment
and Arm code generation. The change adds roughly 154,336 fixed clearing bytes
per normal `find_uv` invocation relative to D3 (the new 77,168-byte extent at
entry and exit): about 154,336 bytes on the frozen KeyGen path and 308,672
bytes across the two sequential invocations on the frozen Sign path. This must
be included in target timing/energy measurements. It does not change
secret-dependent arithmetic or access order and does not improve constant-time
or SPA properties.

The next candidate/ML2 overlay has stricter effective-type and erasure
obligations. It must use a typed union or equivalent standard-C storage model,
never unchecked character-buffer casts. Before activating the candidate
member, every ML2/retry object must be finalized and cleared, no pointer may
escape into the later phase, and the member transition and addresses must be
parameter/phase dependent rather than secret dependent. Canary tests must
exercise both members and failure during each phase. A smaller workspace does
not itself imply less leakage; reuse may change power coupling and remanence.

D5 commit `2771afa…` satisfies those typed-lifetime obligations for the three
early `find_uv` ML2 entries. Core, retry permutation and unpublished output
remain disjoint inside one struct; that complete object is the union member
overlaid with later candidates. Every return clears the active state and the
phase transition clears the complete union. The fixed addresses and sizes add
no secret-dependent allocation choice, but retries and arithmetic remain
variable-time and reuse can change power coupling. No constant-time or SPA
claim follows.

D6 commit `15a69ee…` reuses the same ML2 member sequentially for both
post-`find_uv` fixed-degree reductions. It adds no secret-dependent storage
choice, and independent tests require full-workspace clearing and output
nonpublication on the covered failures. It does add repeated fixed-size
clears. For one successful fixed-degree call with `A` ML2 attempts, the
explicit stack-independent path writes at least
`379,648 + 155,264*A` bytes through its nested clear operations, before normal
workspace initialization; Clapotis invokes the fixed-degree path twice. The
legacy D5 path already performs the per-attempt core clears, so D6's explicit
fixed-degree plumbing adds 379,648 clear bytes per call while removing the
compatibility `find_uv` clear only once per Clapotis invocation. These writes
are fixed-length, but their cycle/energy and data-dependent switching effects
must be measured on Cortex-M33. Production K/S does not yet call the explicit
route, and the shared object retains allocator/legacy branches, so no heap-free
or leakage claim is made for the current Sign ELF.

D7 commit `f6f7bf5…` extends fixed-address retry ownership to compact MLLL
product and intersection APIs. The layout decision remains phase-based rather
than secret-value-based, and audited wrappers clear their complete retry
member on covered normal and failure returns. Nested public wrappers can add
284,736 or 379,648 fixed clear bytes before per-attempt core clearing; the
exact target cost depends on the inherited retry count. Normal LLL, ML2,
prime search and retry behavior remain variable-time and the extra writes may
alter power traces. Production Sign still calls legacy entry points, so D7 is
not evidence for an allocator-free or side-channel-resistant signer.

D8 commit `3ea2b47…` extends the same fixed-address ownership to prime-norm
equivalent-ideal search. The explicit wrapper selects a fixed workspace by
public API choice and independent differential tests preserve output and
post-call RNG state. Prime search, retry count and arithmetic remain
variable-time. Nested entry/retry clearing adds 379,648 fixed logical bytes
per invocation beyond inherited core clears, before ordinary initialization;
this may change timing, energy and power switching even though its length is
not secret-selected. Public KeyGen/Sign still call the legacy entry and both
branches remain in shared objects, so D8 supports neither heap-free nor
side-channel-resistance claims.

D9 commit `cb04091…` adds the same fixed-address ownership choice to the final
ideal construction in prime and composite/cofactor random-ideal sampling.
Fixed CTR-DRBG differentials show identical exact ideal representation and
post-call RNG state; NULL workspace fails before consuming RNG. For a

are logical lengths rather than cycles or bus transactions. Public KeyGen and

D10 commit `7b549db…` makes the arena choice at a separate encoded KeyGen API,
not from a secret value, and sequentially reuses the same fixed address for D9,

## Randomness architecture

Test and production RNGs are separate interfaces.

### Deterministic test RNG

The host/target KAT build accepts an explicit seed and reproduces the oracle stream. It is clearly labeled, excluded from production builds, and used for byte-for-byte differential tests and failure replay.

### Production RNG adapter

The RP2350 production adapter remains unimplemented and unaudited. Before any production-security statement it must establish:

- the exact hardware entropy source and its documented operating/health conditions;
- startup and continuous health tests, failure propagation and no silent fallback;
- conditioning/DRBG construction with domain separation and reseed policy;
- behavior across reset, sleep, clock changes and fault conditions;
- protection against accidental deterministic-test configuration;
- independent review of the complete entropy path.

The presence of a hardware random source alone is not a security argument.

## Target measurement leakage

Instrumentation changes timing and may itself expose secrets. Deterministic research firmware may report phase IDs, retry counts and memory canaries, but production firmware must not emit secret-derived traces. Benchmark serial/USB output occurs outside timed cryptographic regions and is disabled for side-channel experiments.

Host-side `ctgrind`/Timecop/dudect-style testing can detect some regressions but cannot establish power resistance on the Cortex-M33. If a constant-time profile is pursued, target trace acquisition and leakage assessment are separate required experiments.

## D12c host timing-leakage screen

A reproducible fixed-vs-random experiment now provides positive evidence that
the current Sign implementation is observably variable time on the frozen
host. It fixes one key and message, alternates AB/BA execution order and varies
only the deterministic signing-RNG stream. A fixed-vs-fixed dataset is run as
a negative control. Verification occurs outside both timed regions, and every
signature is checked. The measured binary enables ML2 counters, so it is a
diagnostic build rather than a production artifact.

Two 64-pair runs reverse the dataset order. Their controls stay below the
exploratory `|t| > 4.5` threshold: first-/second-order Welch values are
`-0.002/0.768` and `0.249/-0.204`. The primary fixed-vs-random datasets give
`3.590/-5.393` and `3.582/-5.386`, so both independently trigger on the
centered-square second-order statistic. The random-class sample standard
deviation is 161.59x and 121.37x the fixed class. For the same 64 random
streams, elapsed time repeats with Pearson `0.999958` and Spearman `0.999634`
correlation across runs; signatures and recorded path counters are exact.

This is a timing-leakage detection result, not a key-recovery result. The
coarse ML2 attempt count is only 17 or 18 and has Pearson correlation near
zero (`-0.0226`/`-0.0232`), so it does not localize the source. The existing
retry, Cornacchia, division/GCD, lattice, theta and early-success surfaces
remain candidates. The 64-pair host result neither proves the absence or
presence of exploitable RP2350 power/EM leakage nor establishes a
constant-time profile. An uninstrumented target image, external trigger and
independent fixed-vs-random trace acquisitions remain required. Exact design,
raw rows, measured binaries and validation commands are frozen under
`experiments/sca/` and `results/host/`.

## RP2350 triggered modular-exponentiation result

The D12c Level-I/RADIX32 `ibz_pow_mod` implementation uses an ordinary
exponent-bit branch: each set exponent bit performs an additional modular
multiplication. A separate Pico 2 diagnostic image fixes a 521-bit prime and
base, varies only a same-length exponent, disables interrupts, and drives GPIO
2 high only around the linked production primitive. The final disassembly gate
checks interrupt disable, GPIO set, exactly one `ibz_pow_mod` call, GPIO clear,
and interrupt restoration in order. USB work remains outside the window.

Two reversed-order 32-pair acquisitions keep the identical-input A/B controls
stable (`t1/t2=-0.518/-0.144` and `-0.603/-1.405`). Fixed-vs-random two-class
statistics do not cross 4.5 because the fixed exponent's weight lies near the
random mean. Within each random class, however, exponent weight explains timing
almost perfectly: Pearson `0.999860`, slopes `4,004.02/4,004.06 µs/set bit`,
and R² `0.999720` in both runs. The same 32 random-exponent times correlate at
`0.999999999` across runs, with 2-µs median and 5-µs maximum absolute
difference. Independent two-bit versus 521-bit diagnostics give about
`4,011 µs` per added set bit. This is a positive target timing-dependence
finding.

It is not an analog power/EM result, a single-trace exponent recovery, or a
full-Sign key-recovery result. The harness uses controlled public experimental
inputs and exposes only digests after the trigger. GPIO instrumentation itself
is diagnostic and must not enter production. Full-Sign execution remains far
too slow for a naive thousands-trace campaign, so external acquisition should
first use this trigger with segmented or reduced-width calibration, then move
to coarse phase triggers and independently repeated fixed/control campaigns.
The frozen firmware, CSVs and recomputable report are under
`results/rp2350/`.

## First exponent-branch mitigation and residual leakage

Because the legacy result is positive, side-channel resistance is now a
mandatory independent gate. The fail-closed state machine in
`experiments/sca/resistance-plan.json` keeps deployment status at
`combined_controls_integrated_residual_control_and_address_differences_reproduced_physical_pending`;
fixed layout and allocator removal cannot satisfy the timing, structural,
variable-integer-control, analog, attack-recovery or full-Sign gates.

The combined protected Sign route has now been measured twice with exact
PC-table/DWARF edge attribution and again with selected-function effective-
address callbacks. The fixed/fixed control is stable, but all 4/4
fixed/random pairs show reproducible edge differences and all 4/4 selected
address prefixes differ. The first sources are the ideal-construction
bit-length maximum scan and generic integer sign/used-limb paths in
`ibz_bitsize`/`ibz_mul`. This is positive structural evidence against a
whole-Sign constant-time claim. It is not a physical trace, a demonstration
that the long-term key is recoverable, or proof that those first differences
are the most exploitable leakage source. The frozen evidence is checked by
`scripts/check_sca_combined_residual_trace.py`.

The plan separates structural constant-work review (SCA-8) from empirical
leakage testing. A production signer must remove or protect secret-dependent
control flow, loop counts, address sequences and table/index choices over the
complete Sign path. This is required even if a finite timing or TVLA-style
campaign reports no leakage. It is still insufficient by itself, because
operands can leak through power/EM even when the instruction and address
schedules are fixed.

The first countermeasure candidate replaces the 521-bit diagnostic's
square-and-multiply control flow by a fixed 521-iteration
square-and-multiply-always schedule. Both results are computed and a full
27-limb mask selects the exponent-bit result. Host differential/sanitizer tests
pass 68 cases. The linked Cortex-M33 loop has two modular-multiply call sites,
one public limb-loop branch, no legacy-powmod edge and static 1,128-/232-byte
candidate/helper frames.

Two reversed 32-pair target runs reduce the absolute random-exponent
weight/time slope from about 4,004 to 18.21--18.23 µs per set bit, at least a
219.66x reduction. R² falls from 0.99972 to about 0.0172; the low/high-weight
diagnostic slope falls by at least 333.82x. This is evidence that the direct
extra-multiplication-count channel has been substantially mitigated at the
coarse timer resolution.

It is not evidence of constant time. Same-input candidate timings still
correlate at 0.9999987 across runs, and random-input sample SD remains at least
680x fixed-input SD. The candidate deliberately calls variable-time
`ibz_mul`, `ibz_mod` and division; mask-selected data can also remain visible
to power/EM. It costs 1.33872x median time on the measured random exponents and
is not integrated into Sign. Therefore no analog-SPA, exponent-recovery,
full-Sign or production-resistance claim follows. Fixed-work reduction and
analog/attack-driven experiments remain required.

## Fixed-work arithmetic mitigation and residual class leakage

The second diagnostic implements that fixed-work arithmetic step for a narrow
521-bit domain. Each powmod has 521 exponent rounds, 1,042 modular
multiplications, 542,882 multiplier-bit rounds and 1,085,764 full 17-word
modular additions. Source and linked-object audits find no secret-bit branch,
division, used-limb scan, lookup table or legacy arithmetic edge in the timed
primitive. This removes the measured operation-count mechanism; it does not
remove data-dependent switching or prove anything about the complete signer.

Two reversed 32-pair RP2350 runs reduce the absolute legacy exponent-weight
slope by at least 41,843.94x. The candidate costs only about 1.02426x the
legacy median and is about 0.76510x the first countermeasure. However, the
fixed/random paired mean is `-5.1875` and `-4.96875 µs` in the two runs; the
pooled median is `-5 µs` and paired `t=-7.7963`. The class offset therefore
reproduces after reversing dataset and pair order. Control execution-position
and variance alerts further prevent a clean negative interpretation.

The fail-closed outcome is consequently unchanged: the coarse timer screen is
positive, constant time is not established, and analog SPA/EM, published
exponent recovery and full-Sign resistance are untested. In particular, this
experiment does not rebut the attack in
[ePrint 2025/830](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/830); it only demonstrates that
one known coarse operation-count channel can be reduced dramatically with a
small local time/code penalty. The protected arithmetic must next be integrated
at the attacked Cornacchia path, followed by analog traces and an explicit
attack-recovery attempt.

## Executed Cornacchia attack surface and width proof

The attacked route has now been observed inside the frozen D13 signer.  Across
twelve deterministic Sign invocations, source-level instrumentation records
361 calls from `quat_represent_integer` into `ibz_cornacchia_prime` and its
modular square root.  Calls per Sign range from 13 to 72.  Moduli range from
360 to 378 bits; 190 are `5 mod 8`, giving two-adicity two and the direct
relation `q=(m-1)/4`, `(q+1)/2=(m+3)/8` used by the published analysis.  The
other 171 are `1 mod 8`; observed two-adicity reaches 12.  Thus merely fixing
the exponent-bit branch does not fix the Tonelli--Shanks iteration trace.

This corpus is not treated as an all-input bound.  The frozen Level-I source
and compiled constants instead give a conservative 492-bit upper bound for
production Cornacchia moduli.  The fixed-degree route uses
`L <= TORSION_EVEN_POWER-HD_extra_torsion = 246` and adjusted target
`4*u*(2^L-u) < 2^492`.  The random-aux route combines a positive 252-bit
cofactor, a norm below `2^126`, and the standard-order factor four, yielding a
target below `2^380`.  Cornacchia then subtracts a positive term.  The 521-bit
fixed-work primitive therefore has 29 bits of Level-I width margin.

That result authorizes only a checked integration experiment.  It does not
cover Levels III/V or generic powmod, and it does not authorize a
variable-time fallback if the runtime guard fails.  It also does not make
Tonelli--Shanks, primality testing, the varying number of Cornacchia attempts,
or the rest of Sign constant-work.  The frozen profile explicitly keeps
`published_attack_blocked=false` and `analog_spa_resistance_established=false`.
The artifacts and fail-closed checks are
`results/host/sca-d13-cornacchia-{profile,bound}.json` and
`scripts/check_cornacchia_sca_profile.py`.

## Integrated Cornacchia fixed-work boundary

Experimental commit `c2a80712e32891c5228da3f49f0148993a4ec560` connects the
521-bit fixed-work arithmetic to the exact Level-I production Cornacchia
square-root adapter. A valid protected call never falls back to `ibz_pow_mod`,
`ibz_mul_mod_positive`, or the legacy square root. A width or domain violation
is fatal rather than silently selecting the leaky path. The Cortex-M33 object
gate confirms fixed 521-round exponent and multiplier loops, two modular
multiplications per exponent round, two full 17-word modular additions per
multiplier round, and no exponent-bit conditional branch in the protected
arithmetic object.

The frozen twelve-seed corpus executes 361 protected square roots. Every call
succeeds; complete signed-message output and the first 64 bytes drawn from the
RNG after each Sign are byte-identical to the D13 control. Direct legacy versus
protected square-root tests cover residues, aliases, no-root, over-width,
canaries, transactional rejection and full workspace clearing; Release and
fatal ASan+UBSan runs pass. The aggregate host smoke takes 7.94 seconds for
control and 75.01 seconds for protected Sign (`9.447x`). It is one unpaired
host run, not target timing and not an equivalence test.

This evidence removes the published exponent-bit **control-flow mechanism**;
it does not establish that the published key-recovery attack is blocked.
Tonelli--Shanks outer/inner counts, quadratic-non-residue search, `p-1`
factorization, Cornacchia Euclid/half-GCD, retry counts, operand-dependent
switching and the rest of Sign remain variable or untested. In particular,
fixed instruction/address schedules can still leak values through power or EM.
The deployment decision therefore remains `known_leakage`, constant time and
analog resistance remain false, and a failed recovery experiment would only
be bounded empirical evidence.

The frozen artifact checker is
`scripts/check_cornacchia_fixedwork_integration.py`; a full clean rebuild is
`scripts/run_cornacchia_fixedwork_integration.sh`. The next analog campaign is
preregistered in `experiments/sca/cornacchia-analog-plan.json` and has not yet
been executed.

A dedicated RP2350 calibration image has now been built, final-ELF audited,
flashed and exercised on five public fixtures. All five roots, PSP calls and
4,344-byte clear checks passed. Coarse target time nevertheless ranged from
16.004361 to 70.418552 seconds, while the identical-input A/B pair took
32.024229/32.024063 seconds. This is not an analog acquisition, but it
directly confirms that the integrated checkpoint remains variable-time after
the exponent-bit branch is removed. The record therefore keeps
`analog_capture_run=false` and `side_channel_resistance_established=false`.
Exact reconstruction yields 5,210--22,924 fixed-base modular multiplications
for the five records and explains elapsed time at Pearson
`0.999999999981`. The remaining coarse leak is therefore causally tied to
variable non-residue-search and Tonelli--Shanks operation counts, rather than
being dismissed as measurement noise.

### Bounded-outer and fixed-input-reduction WIP

The subsequent WIP replaces that variable outer schedule with 32 bounded
Tonelli--Shanks rounds, scans a fixed list of 32 public non-residue prime
candidates, uses full 17-by-17-word Montgomery multiplication, and replaces
the initial signed `ibz_mod` with a 521-round conditional-subtract reduction.
The fixed list is an experimental fail-closed bound, not an unconditional
proof that every valid Level-I modulus has a non-residue in the list.

On the frozen RP2350 public fixtures, the initial fixed-outer image retained a
reproducible low/high class difference: mean H-minus-L `+14.7667 us`, Welch
`t=6.637997`, 29/30 paired differences positive. After fixed signed reduction,
the same two-order 30-pair screen gives `-2.5333 us`, `t=-0.332420`, with 11
positive, 17 negative and two zero pairs. The two order-specific means have
opposite signs, and all 16 fixtures span 49 us around a roughly 768.108 ms
mean. This removes the previously resolved local timing effect in this corpus;
it is not a timing-equivalence test and does not override the positive
whole-Sign timing evidence above.

The exact flashed UF2 is
`0898c6579cecf33f32715b45dbb5accda73d4e518b33b2b23f74a1001db7110a`.
Its archived ELF gate confirms one IRQ-masked/GPIO-triggered square root, fixed
521-round exponent and signed-reduction loops, no legacy sqrt/pow/div fallback,
and a 4,488-byte workspace clear. The target timer is not a power/EM channel.
Square-and-multiply-always still performs a bit-controlled masked selection and

activity even with identical instruction counts.

The primary acquisition image now narrows GPIO 2 to exactly one of those
protected exponentiations.  Modulus parsing, derivation of the public-fixture
Legendre exponent `(p-1)/2`, Montgomery-context setup, result validation,

`1f8b6ddc81acd98ebc6667d89135c1080de48a29953e4dd3a9e00d2d0c95681e`.
The final-ELF audit finds one protected-pow edge, fixed 521-round structure,

Sixteen public fixtures pass result and clear checks; a ten-cycle alternating

weight Welch statistic is `-0.579`.  This is a small, integer-microsecond
timer screen, not an equivalence test.  It neither observes nor excludes the
bit-selected register/memory transitions, carry propagation, multiplier

Attack evaluation is fail-closed against frozen public ground truth.  All 16
367-bit exponents are independently recomputed from the archived primes,
padded to the 521-round schedule, and matched to the firmware's 17-word FNV
digests.  `scripts/score_sca_exponent_recovery.py` now rejects classifier CSV

it does not itself execute an attack or protect a secret.

The residual risk is also machine-audited rather than left implicit.
`experiments/sca/residual-surface-inventory.json` freezes twelve open
surfaces and their required closure evidence.  At the attacked pow locus, the
source constructs a 32-bit all-zero/all-one selector word directly from each
exponent bit.  The frozen Cortex-M33 compiler output keeps that bit live and
implements selection with multiplication, AND and OR on `bit` and `bit-1`.
The selected exponent-prefix state then feeds the next Montgomery square and
multiply.  An ideal source-level observation of that selector word recovers
all 521 scheduled bits for all 16 public fixtures.  This is not a physical
leakage measurement, but it proves that square-and-multiply-always is neither
first-order masking nor value hiding.  `scripts/check_sca_residual_surfaces.py`
therefore fails if the operation-count mitigation is relabeled as analog
resistance, if any open surface is silently closed, or if the frozen source
and selector model diverge.

directly rather than inferred only from source prose.  In the normal D13
snapshot, fresh Arm GNU 15.2.1 objects retain the complete
`quat_represent_integer -> ibz_cornacchia_prime -> ibz_sqrt_mod_p ->
ibz_pow_mod` chain.  The exponentiation loop is bounded by the secret-derived

361 square roots and 2,180 exponentiations, or 75--409 exponentiations per
Sign.  That is an observation budget, not a security result: the experimental
selector and prefix-dependent operands are still unmasked, and neither the
paper's physical exponent recovery nor a current-C signing-key recovery has
been reproduced or blocked.  The algebraic stages have been replayed: the
paper's three relations recover 24/24 frozen public-fixture Cornacchia inputs,
the isolated current-C Legendre exponent recovers 16/16 fixture moduli through
`m=2e+1`, and a pinned Sage 10.7 run of the authors' 54-bit toy code reaches a

### Why standalone exponent blinding is not adopted

The paper recommends regular exponentiation plus exponent randomization, but
adding a random multiple of the group order is not automatically a proof of
security.  On the attacked affine exponent paths, write
`e=(p+k1)/k2` and consider `E=e+r*(p-1)`.  If SPA recovers the blinded `E`,
then exactly

```text
k2*E-(k1+1) = (1+k2*r)*(p-1).
```

The direct affine recovery of `p` has been replaced by a structured
factorization problem, not erased.  In particular, the entropy of `r` is not
itself an attack-work lower bound.  The frozen heuristic comparison gives a
128-bit blind only about 40.7 bits in the leading ECM term for a blind-sized
factor.  Even the table's first row above that 128-bit proxy, an 896-bit
blind, expands the fixed exponent schedule from 521 to 1,408 rounds and uses
about 20.35 KiB of fresh randomness per Sign on the measured average corpus;
that row is still not a security proof.  Constructing `r*(p-1)` is also a new
power/EM surface involving the same secret-derived `p`.

Independent fresh blinds do not solve the algebraic problem.  One executed
Tonelli--Shanks square root performs a Legendre power, `z^q`,
`n^((q+1)/2)`, and `n^q` under the same `p`.  If SPA recovers those four
blinded exponents exactly, each public affine transformation is a multiple of
the same `p-1`; their GCD is directly `p-1` whenever the small multiplier GCD
is one.  Across 4,096 deterministic trials for each width, this direct result
occurred 4,017 times (98.0713%) with 128-bit blinds and 4,032 times (98.4375%)
with 896-bit blinds.  This is a conditional algebraic attack experiment, not
evidence that current physical traces recover four exponents.

`experiments/sca/cornacchia-exponent-blinding-design.json` and
`scripts/check_cornacchia_exponent_blinding.py` machine-check the algebra,
width/randomness arithmetic, 349,568 reduced modular cases, all 16 frozen
acquisition fixtures and the multi-call GCD model.  Their deliberate decision is rejection pending a
reviewed leakage model and cryptanalysis.  The implementation therefore still
contains no approved exponent/base blinding or arithmetic/Boolean masking,
and the published attack is not considered blocked.

### Why naive two-share selector masking is not adopted

[On the Cost of Lazy Engineering for Masked Software Implementations](https://eprint.iacr.org/2014/413)
formalizes the difference between value- and transition-based leakage.
[Threshold Implementations in Software](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1546)
extends share-placement reasoning to scalar microarchitectures and includes an
ARM Cortex-M4 evaluation, while
[Efficient Second-Order Masked Software Implementations of Ascon](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/755)
demonstrates share-rotation, final-implementation verification and physical
testing.  These are design principles, not drop-in SQIsign Montgomery gadgets.

Splitting the 32-bit selector as `share0=S` and
`share1=S XOR (0-b)` makes either share, viewed alone, uniformly distributed
and gives identical per-share Hamming-weight distributions for `b=0` and
`b=1`.  That marginal property is insufficient on a serial microcontroller.
If the same register or data bus processes `share0` followed by `share1`, the
transition Hamming distance is exactly zero for `b=0` and 32 for `b=1`; it
recovers the selector bit perfectly under that leakage model.

`cornacchia-selector-masking-design.json` and its checker exhaust all 16,380
assignments for word widths 1 through 12 and verify the same full-word
identity symbolically.  This rejects only the naive sequential two-share
schedule.  It is not a claim that all two-share gadgets fail or that the
transition is physically observable on the current board.  A viable masked
implementation must use reviewed composable gadgets, protect the
exponent-prefix-dependent arithmetic state, freeze and inspect share placement
in final Cortex-M33 assembly, and pass the transition-aware `ANALOG-4`
campaign.  No such implementation exists yet.

### Why the secret Montgomery context is part of the masking boundary

Selector masking is not the whole problem.  On the attacked signing route the
temporary Cornacchia modulus is secret-derived.  The frozen 521-bit source
loads it into `context->modulus`, derives the low-word Montgomery inverse,
computes `R mod M` and `R^2 mod M`, and then reuses the unshared modulus and
inverse in every word-wise reduction.  The same execution exposes unshared
base and exponent words, the exponent bit, selector, accumulator prefix,
square/multiply candidates, schoolbook product, reduction factor, carry chain,
borrow/difference and reduced output.  Fixed work removes an operation-count
channel but does not make those values or their transitions secret-independent.

`experiments/sca/cornacchia-masked-montgomery-scope.json` binds this statement
to the exact archived C/header hashes and records an 18-node dependency graph
from the temporary modulus, base/radicand and exponent.  Its checker rejects
six incomplete scopes: schedule-only, selector-only, exponent-only,
accumulator-only, context-only and base-only.  In particular, sharing an
accumulator while multiplying it by an unshared secret modulus is not accepted
as masked Montgomery arithmetic.

A viable candidate must protect or independently hide context construction,
521-bit add/subtract/select/multiply/reduction state, exponent and base
conversion, and the surrounding Tonelli--Shanks powers, updates, retries and
publication path.  It must also preserve share separation in final Cortex-M33
assembly and pass first-/higher-order and attack-driven physical experiments.
[Bitslicing Arithmetic/Boolean Masking Conversions](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/158)
provides composable masking and transition-engineering principles, but its
small-modulus lattice-KEM gadgets are not a drop-in construction for this
secret dynamic-modulus SQIsign computation.  No complete candidate currently
exists, so `published_attack_blocked=false` remains mandatory.

### Reduced arithmetic-masking direction and its transition boundary

`experiments/sca/cornacchia-masked-arithmetic-design.json` compares a full
Boolean circuit, word-level arithmetic sharing with Boolean boundaries,
algebraic blinding alone, and two sequential unshared executions.  Its checker
exhausts 32 two-share secure-AND assignments and 256 masked full-adder
assignments.  Correctness and every individual-wire marginal pass in the
abstract value-probing model.  This is not sufficient on a Cortex-M33: the
Hamming distance from share 0 to share 1 exactly reconstructs the corresponding
input, output, sum or carry bit in the modeled schedules.

A deliberately direct 17-by-32-bit Boolean schoolbook proxy would consume
192,728,320 fresh random bytes (183.800 MiB) for the 1,042 Montgomery
multiplications in one fixed power call, before reduction, context formation,
refresh or transition hardening.  This is neither a lower bound nor an estimate
for an optimized arithmetic-sharing design; it is evidence that the full
Boolean reference should remain a correctness oracle rather than the first
production candidate.

The first reduced experiment now implements a two-limb arithmetic sharing over
`Z/(2^64)`: share-wise addition, refresh and randomized ring multiplication.
Release and fatal ASan/UBSan runs pass 64 edge pairs and 200,000 deterministic
random pairs; exhaustive rings from one through four bits cover another
1,082,400 assignments.  Every individual modeled wire remains
secret-independent, but five direct adjacent-share channels are
secret-dependent at every checked width.  A zero-precharge abstraction removes
those modeled HD channels; it does not model the Cortex-M33 pipeline, ALU,
register file or buses.

Consequently the C reference remains a functional oracle only.  The first
hand-controlled subprimitive is now frozen: a 66-byte/23-instruction
Cortex-M33 arithmetic-share refresh.  Its exact instruction sequence passes
36 edge cases and 10,000 deterministic cases under QEMU, and 299,592 reduced
assignments show that the direct adjacent-share schedule leaks while the
modeled zero-precharge schedule does not.  An exact-order model additionally
checks 37,448 reduced assignments over all
55 adjacent ALU A/B/result, selected-register-write and load/store-data
transitions; direct ALU, load and store share-to-share mutations are rejected.
QEMU and these models do not cover register-file coupling, pipeline, bus,
interrupt or analog leakage, so this is not a physical or
composable masking result.  The byte-identical RP2350 fixture now exists: its
ELF/UF2 are frozen, it has been flashed, and 12 serial records verify the share
formulas, recombination, scratch erasure and full context clearing.  Every
A/B/R class executes the same three RNG calls, and caller-saved `r12` is
zeroed before GPIO-high.  The RNG, setup, checks, clear and serial output are
outside the IRQ-disabled GPIO window.  This removes two acquisition-history
confounds; it is not a masking proof.  No analog samples have been acquired.
`ANALOG-5` therefore still
requires independent masks, first-/second-order characterization and direct
HD attacks before the primitive can be reused.  The dedicated dataset
template and scorer now bind the exact firmware, validate per-trace share
formulas and balanced pair ordering, compute pointwise first-order and
centered-square TVLA plus a preregistered cross-time centered-product
second-order screen over two disjoint public-timing windows, and retain twelve
secret-attack/diagnostic
HW/HD/carry/borrow models plus two permuted null controls.  Their synthetic
injected-leakage test
passes only as tooling validation and is mechanically ineligible as physical
evidence.  The fail-closed 2026-08-23 readiness record detects the flashed

analog samples remain zero.  Boolean
carry/borrow/comparison/selection boundaries and masked multiplication remain
unimplemented.  Every non-linear composition needs fresh randomness, and the

The physical campaign remains pending because the current machine has only the

## Certified norm-sketch research boundary

The D13 research checkpoint replaces resident arbitrary-precision `find_uv`
norm rows with fixed-size ordering sketches and exact replay. This does not
truncate a value used by the protocol: unequal `(bit length, leading 64 bits)`
pairs certify exact order, and every equal sketch regenerates both full norms
before deciding. Search operands are also regenerated exactly. A deliberate
`2^100`/`2^100+1` collision fixture and frozen transcript tests cover the
fallback and end-to-end output preservation.

The fixed storage shape is not a constant-time construction. The number of
exact comparison fallbacks, the number of search norm evaluations,
invertibility failures and first success all depend on intermediate values.
The first corpus observes a low sort fallback rate (about 0.28%), but search
replay ranges are visibly variable. These counts can alter timing and power
traces even though allocation size and addresses do not change. D13 therefore
requires new fixed-vs-random host and RP2350 screens; no side-channel property
is inherited from D12c or inferred from the SRAM reduction.

The renewed D13 host screen is now complete.  Two reversed 64-pair runs retain
stable fixed-vs-fixed controls and reproduce a centered-square alert
(`t2=-5.410/-5.374`) in the fixed-vs-random comparison.  Same-seed timings
correlate at Pearson `0.999929` across runs.  A separate D13 profile observes
5,041 exact sketch ties in 2,062,361 sort comparisons (`0.2444%`) and variable
search replay, but those counts correlate only weakly with elapsed Sign time.
The result therefore shows that D13 remains embedded in a measurably
variable-time signer; it neither establishes that the sketch causes the
dominant signal nor improves the existing leakage.  The physical RP2350
`ibz_pow_mod` finding also remains applicable because D13 does not change that
primitive.  There is still no D13 full-Sign analog power/EM result, key-recovery
result, or constant-time claim.

The final D13 link reduces the guarded operation owner by 180,928 bytes and
leaves 211,072 bytes unassigned, but this storage result has no constant-time
interpretation. Exact-tie replay, regenerated search norms, retries and
first-success control flow remain input dependent. The ELF policy proves only
the frozen deterministic closure's absence of heap, GMP, system RNG and
curated legacy stack paths; it does not prove that timing, power or EM traces
are independent of secrets.

Two frozen D13 board boots complete the same K/S/V input and preserve the
expected digests, cleanup, Verify RNG-nonuse and PSP/MSP extents. Their elapsed
times are repetitions of one deterministic sample, not a leakage or input-
distribution test. In particular, the first-boot Sign time is 1.037312 times
the archived D12c value while the repeated host smoke did not show a
consistent Sign slowdown. Neither observation establishes equivalence,
causality, a worst-case stack bound or a secret-independent trace; broader
target inputs and analog acquisition remain required for stronger statements.

## v3 RP2350 fixed-key timing boundary

The preregistered target screen now has a terminal result. Ten official
`p324_3` key pairs were copied one at a time into the same fixed-address RAM
buffer. The message and signing-RNG stream were fixed, XIP cache state was
invalidated immediately before each timed Sign and Verify, and two independent
deterministic key orders were each repeated five times. The official and
lifetime-overlay images each produced 100 valid signatures; all 200 verified
and retained stable per-key signature digests and intact PSP canaries.

Both images satisfy the predeclared detection rule. Their between-pass
Spearman rank correlations are 1.0, and their per-pass fastest-to-slowest key
median spans are 50.8419--51.3752% of the central median, far above ten times
the median within-key MAD. The lifetime-overlay image also reproduces the
3,928-byte Sign PSP reduction in all 100 paired samples.

This is positive evidence of repeatable **key-associated RP2350 wall-clock
timing** under one finite design. Each changed key pair contains both public
and secret components, so secret-only causation is not isolated. The result is
not an RP2350 control/address trace, analog current/EM leakage, key recovery,
population or worst-case timing, or resistance evidence. The fixed outer
image order also makes the observed inter-implementation speed difference
descriptive only. `physical_leakage_established`,
`key_recovery_established`, and `side_channel_resistance_established` remain
false. The 2026-09-04 hardware inventory records zero physical traces because
no current/EM probe or scope/SCA acquisition instrument is present.

## Fault and input handling

Verify processes attacker-controlled encodings. Existing length/canonicality checks must remain before shifts, loops or workspace indexing. Workspace refactors require fuzzing malformed public keys/signatures and verifying that no failure path reads uninitialized or out-of-range memory.

KeyGen/Sign arithmetic overflow checks in the frozen fixed-width implementation are part of correctness and must not be compiled away as assertions. A precision failure must be a hard operation failure, never silent truncation or a retry presented as ordinary randomness.
